Eighteen accents and nineteen ways to set a label

Purple and yellow turned out to be the most interesting request, because the pair does something the single-hue options cannot: purple carries the text and the yellow only shows up where something is marked. That is what a person does to a page when reading closely, so it is the one palette here that says something about the research rather than merely coordinating with it.

Every specimen is your own nav bar, news row and work card. The TIME link in each is shown already highlighted, so you can see what the hover tint does before you commit to it. Contrast is measured live from the rendered pixels.

Purple and yellow

Five ways to run the pair. The first four use yellow for the rules and underlines; the last uses it only as a highlight, leaving purple to do everything else.

Greens

From the last round, teal end to moss end, with one desaturated outlier.

Blues

Four blues that are not the default product blue. Each comes from a different making tradition rather than from a UI kit.

The materials of thinking

Sampled from the physical apparatus of reading, annotating and working a problem through. These are the ones that would actually mean something on a site about how people think — and the two-hue pairs are the hardest to mistake for a generated palette.

Label treatments

Nineteen ways to set the news category and the award marker, none of them a pill. Colour is held constant so the treatment is the only variable, and each is shown across three entries — a short category and a long one behave differently, and the award marker has to work too.

Applying whichever you pick

Colour is two values in :root plus the three rgba() tints sharing the strong hue. A two-hue pair needs one extra line, since the tints stay derived from the strong colour while the rules use the soft one.

The label treatment is a single rule, the shared .news .kind, .work .award block. Give me a colour name and a label number and I will apply both.
